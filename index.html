Beyond the build — 04

CFG 2026.

JPMC Code for Good · Hyderabad

I still remember standing outside the JPMorganChase building in Hyderabad for the first time. It was huge. I paused there thinking, “Okay… this is actually happening.” I had goosebumps.

After the briefing and lunch, I met people from different teams and made new friends. An Empathy in AI session introduced us to our mentors, Khushi Aggarwal and Santhosh Munnoor. Our team chose Challenge 1 from The Barabari Collective: evaluate UI/UX and product-design assignments while keeping human judgment in the loop. Conversations with the NGO team and our mentors helped us challenge assumptions and shape a practical approach. There was also a puzzle challenge, dancing, and plenty of food. “Code for Food” made sense.

01 / 07

Then our AI coding agent went off track. While we were away, it changed parts of the codebase, introduced another framework, and sent us chasing hallucinated solutions. We lost around an hour and a half. Around 4 PM, Rashmi K., Ravi Kant Dwivedi, and the SMEs helped us simplify the plan and get back to controlled coding. We recovered in about 40 minutes.

I focused mainly on the student and organization dashboards, and also contributed to model evaluation and integration. After working through merge conflicts, we brought the pieces together, tested the app, and submitted with 10 seconds remaining.

We didn’t win, but I left with an experience I’ll remember. Building a product takes more than code: understanding the problem, adapting when things break, communicating well, using AI responsibly, and staying calm under pressure.

Grateful to Rashmi K., Khushi Aggarwal, Ravi Kant Dwivedi, Santhosh Munnoor, the SMEs and volunteers, and my teammates Snehitha Reddy V. and Charitha Reddy Kalam for making it a real team effort.

From standing outside in awe, to a broken codebase, to rebuilding, to a ten-second submission. What a journey.
